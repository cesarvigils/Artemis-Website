## 1. Verdict per page

**`/` (index)** - The strongest page on the site and the pacing claim in `pass2-report.md` §4/B2 checks out: eight sections, eight distinct compositions (diagonal split hero, data strip, timing table, native filmstrip, four-column hairline teaser, four-across register, logo-led row, full-width statement with a numbered sequence), and a real heading ladder of 80/64/48/36px at 1440 that ladders at 1.25-1.33 all the way down. What lets it down is the millimetre work rather than the structure: the roster teaser's names sit 1px from their column hairlines because a component shorthand out-specifies the page rule meant to inset them; the pillar register's second row is indented 25px and carries an orphan hairline from 641px to 1024px for the same class of reason; the results table has no column gutters, so the Drivers and Date cells run together into one string at 1024 while the GT3 rows leave a 109px hole in the same place; and at 768 the hero puts 521px of hillside above the first word. The section rhythm is also keyed to viewport *height*, so the widest layout gets less air between sections than the tablet does.

**`/team`** - The entry-list aside and the derived "Last drive" line are the right answers and they read well at 390 and 1440. The page falls apart in the 761-1200px band, which is where most tablets and small laptops live: the `4.5rem / 1fr / 1.6fr` driver row gives the name column 197px at 1024 against a 25.8px name, so every single road driver's name breaks across two lines, the entry-strip names break too, and the mono "Last drive" string splits a date in half ("Suzuka 1000, 06 / SEP 2026"). The sticky group rail is correct in principle but at 1024 it is a 269px column holding three short lines beside 460px of nothing. At 768 the page head stacks and everything is comfortable again.

**`/about`** - The quietest and most coherent page: two heading sizes, a 52ch origin measure, and a collage of three genuinely different proportions that collapses sensibly to 2-up under 900px. Two real problems. The `long` pillar variant keeps its two-column grid down to 640px, so at 768 a 23.7px title sits in a 279px cell beside a 388px sentence, and at 1024 the sentence measure jumps to 48.6ch while the title column is still 377px - the two halves never settle into a ratio. And the page lead widows at 390 (5 lines, last line 2 words, 24% fill).

**`/partners`** - The dossier-in-the-first-screen idea works and the offer list's "one headline item then three" is a good break from an equal grid. At 1024 the page head's aside grid squeezes the copy column to 450px while the h1 is still at its 73px step, so the page's own h1 breaks into three lines with "WITH" alone on the middle one, and the lead drops to 33.6ch with a two-word, 27%-fill widow. The offer list at the same width runs 32ch cells under a 52ch full-row item. The roster line is six 22px-tall links at an 8px row gap - the smallest touch targets on the site.

**`404`** - Right in spirit, and the `min(18rem, 40vw)` mark is a genuine improvement on the 6rem version. But the layout breakpoint is at 760px, one notch below the most common portrait-tablet width, so at 768 the two-column split survives into a 193px left column: the mark's SVG box is 192.8 x 288px, meaning the glyph renders at ~190px inside a box with ~50px of dead space above and below it, and the two CTAs stack at mismatched widths (288.8px over 199.8px) because the equal-flex rule also sits behind the 760px query. The page is ~40% empty vertically at that size.

## 4. Do not change

1. **The two-weight hero lockup** (`Hero.astro:47-52, 150-158`). ARTEMIS in Nexa 700 and ESPORTS in Nexa 300 at the same size, tracked +0.06em and set in teal, is the one place where the licensing constraint (two weights only) becomes the idea rather than a limitation. It is also the only intentional 2-line `lastLineWords=1` heading on the site. Leave the sizes, the weights, the tracking and the colour exactly as they are.
2. **Tabular-figure discipline.** I checked every element on the site that renders a number that could align with another: `.hero-pos`, `.hero-pos-field`, `.pos`, `.pos-field`, `.col-date`, `.driver-number` (both variants), `.entry-number`, `.founding dd`, `.step-index`, `.roster-count`, `.also-date` and everything carrying `.data` all set `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`. There is not a single gap. Do not "simplify" `.data` by dropping it.
3. **Icon optical alignment and the single stroke weight.** Measured icon-centre vs box-centre delta is **0.0px** on every `.btn` and **-0.4 to -0.6px** on `.text-link` (`align.txt`, all viewports) - well inside the threshold where nudging would do harm. The icon set is one 1.5px square-cap stroke at 13/14/15/16/18px, each matched to the label beside it. Do not add sizes or start hand-tuning positions.
4. **`--radius: 0` and the hairline ramp.** Three line tokens (`--line`, `--line-strong`, `--line-teal`) used consistently for table heads, ghost borders, chips, group rules and accents, with zero rounded corners anywhere. This is the "pit wall" vocabulary and it is applied without a single exception. D2/D3 are failures *inside* this system, not arguments against it.
5. **`ResultsTable`'s stacked-but-still-a-table behaviour** (`ResultsTable.astro:13-24, 183-238`). Explicit `role="table"/"rowgroup"/"row"/"cell"/"columnheader"` plus the visually hidden `<caption>`, so the `display: block` stack under 860px is still announced as a table; and the `{result.entries && …}` guard so a position prints bare rather than inventing a field size. D21 proposes a new intermediate layout - keep these two properties through it.
